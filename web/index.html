What could not be checked
    This is a self-check made before presentation, from what you typed in. It is not a bank's examination and it is not advice. Whether a presentation complies is determined by the issuing bank on the documents themselves — UCP 600 articles 14(a), 15 and 16.

    Every check this tool runs

    Each one names the sub-article of UCP 600 it rests on. Where a check clears something rather than finding fault, that matters just as much: it stops a document being reprinted for nothing.

    What it does not do

    • It is not an examination. A bank examines the documents themselves, on their face. This reads a description of them that you typed in.
    • It cannot see a signature, a stamp or an original. Articles 17(b) and 17(c) decide originality on the face of the paper.
    • It does not read your documents. There is no scanning and no optical character recognition; every field is entered by hand.
    • It does not cover a transferred credit (article 38), instalment shipments (article 32), standby credits, bank-to-bank reimbursement (article 13) or assignment of proceeds (article 39).
    • It applies no local law. UCP 600 applies because the credit says it does (article 1); the law of the place of presentation sits above it.
    • It is not advice. Confirm every article and figure against the current text of UCP 600, and take a banker's or a lawyer's view before you act on anything that matters.

    The figures these checks apply

    Every figure the tool uses is defined in one place in the source, with the sub-article it comes from. Several of them a credit may vary, and the rules themselves are revised from time to time — so each is editable here. Changing one is recorded in the report, so a report always says which figures produced it.
